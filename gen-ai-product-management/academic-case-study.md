# SkillForge Academy

Build, validate and sell a Data Science + Generative AI course.

*Worked educational example — not a validated business. Sources reviewed 2026-10-18. Prices, personas, targets, RICE inputs and financial results are illustrative.*

## The product

An eight-week, part-time program plus LMS portal for analysts with basic Python and SQL. The business hypothesis is that focused projects and human rubric feedback can make a small paid pilot valuable despite strong established alternatives.

- Duration: 8 weeks
- Workload: 5–7 hours/week
- Pilot capacity: 30 seats
- Price hypothesis: 299 USD
- Prerequisites: Basic Python, SQL and comfort working with tabular data.

## 01 / Draft the course and LMS PRD

*Module 1 · Provisional product definition*

Problem hypothesis: some analysts struggle to connect scattered data-science and GenAI resources into a manageable project path with useful feedback. This must be checked with real learners.

- Primary learner: a working analyst who can already use basic Python and SQL; an early-career learner is secondary, not a zero-to-expert promise.
- Value proposition: a bounded eight-week sequence, a data project and a GenAI evaluation capstone, reviewed with a visible rubric.
- MVP: an outcome-led course page, prerequisite check, enrollment/access, accessible lessons, progress, quizzes, project submission and staffed feedback.
- Non-goals: accredited degree, guaranteed job placement, comprehensive advanced ML, enterprise SSO, a custom LMS engine or an unrestricted AI tutor.
- Build/buy: begin on an existing LMS with proven access, payments, assignments and gradebook; customize the proposition and learning service before rebuilding commodity infrastructure.

| Metric | Proposed pilot target | Measurement and caveat |
|---|---|---|
| Demand | At least 10 paid commitments before full production | An experiment gate, not observed results; disclose offer, start date and refund terms. |
| 7-day activation | ≥70% of non-refunded paid seats | At least one lesson and first assessment attempted within seven days of access. |
| Learning success | ≥60% reviewed capstone completion | Use a mature cohort after eight weeks; apply a consistent rubric. |
| Refunds | ≤8% of paid seats | Full-refund count divided by paid seats; understand reasons, not just the number. |
| Commercial viability | Positive incremental contribution | Include acquisition, delivery, processing and fixed campaign/content costs; this does not equal accounting profit. |

**Decision / next validation:** Interview 12 target learners and three potential employer buyers before finalizing the promise. All targets above are hypotheses.

## 02 / Read demand signals and competitors

*Module 2 · Sources checked; product demand still unvalidated*

There is evidence to investigate the category. It does not establish the reachable audience, acceptable price or conversion of this particular course.

- WEF's employer-skills outlook places AI/data capabilities among growing skill priorities. This is employer expectation, not paid-course demand.
- The BLS page reviewed on 18 Oct 2026 projects U.S. data-scientist employment growth of 35% for 2025–2035. This occupation-specific projection is not a global course market size.
- Coursera's September 2026 report describes substantial GenAI learning activity across Coursera/Udemy data. Enrollment events are not unique paying buyers and cannot be combined directly with employment statistics.
- Positioning hypothesis: a focused analyst pathway with evaluated projects and responsive human feedback. Established catalogs already cover these skills, so differentiation must be tested.
- No TAM, market-share, competitor-price or willingness-to-pay number has been invented. Obtain regional checkout prices and a dated reachable-audience estimate before pricing decisions.

| Alternative | Verified/observed proposition | Question for SkillForge |
|---|---|---|
| IBM Data Science / Coursera | Official page describes a data-science professional certificate and practical/AI skills. | Does a shorter analyst-specific pathway add enough value? |
| DataCamp | Official track describes an Associate Data Scientist in Python pathway. | Would mentor-reviewed work matter more than a large interactive catalog? |
| DeepLearning.AI | Official catalog search showed applied AI, analytics and GenAI topics; detailed retrieval was limited. | How will the course stay narrower and avoid duplicating strong specialist content? |
| Free docs/video self-study | A relevant alternative to test in learner interviews; no completion or price claims assumed. | What feedback, structure or accountability would justify payment? |

**Decision / next validation:** Create a sourced competitor sheet with date, geography, pricing basis and limits. Test the differentiator with recent course purchasers.

## 03 / Turn market trends into product changes

*Module 2 · Evidence → interpretation → experiment*

The appropriate response to a trend is a testable product decision, not a feature list copied from competitors.

| Signal | Interpretation / hypothesis | Curriculum or portal response | Validation |
|---|---|---|---|
| AI and data skills feature in employer priorities | Practical workflow relevance may matter. | Teach data validation and GenAI evaluation together. | Ask learners to show an actual work task; review a sample project. |
| Platform interest includes applied AI topics | Advanced terminology may attract attention without readiness. | Show prerequisites and a sample lab before enrollment. | Observe whether prospects can complete a short prerequisite task. |
| Strong existing catalogs and credentials | More content is unlikely to be enough differentiation. | Test human rubric feedback and a coherent small path. | Compare two honestly described offers; measure commitment and support cost. |
| LMS documentation includes assignments, gradebook and progress | Core learning infrastructure can be bought/configured. | Pilot on an existing LMS; defer a custom portal build. | Time-box setup and test the full learner journey before selling. |

**Decision / next validation:** PRD revision: make prerequisite transparency, an applied capstone and measurable feedback quality core. Defer enterprise features and AI tutoring until pilot evidence supports them.

These personas organize research hypotheses. They are not observed users, representative samples or validated demand.

- Recruit by recent behavior and prerequisite readiness rather than decorative demographics.
- Run 30-minute interviews and a short usability test; record consent and de-identify notes.
- Keep learner, buyer and administrator needs separate. Link decisions to actual quotes and counterexamples.

| Persona | Job and likely constraint | LMS need to investigate | Neutral research question |
|---|---|---|---|
| Working analyst | Apply AI to existing analytical work; studies around a full-time job. | Clear weekly workload, resume learning, job-relevant projects. | Tell me about the last work task that made you look for a course. |
| Early-career learner | Build credible project evidence; uncertain readiness and budget. | Prerequisite check, worked examples, transparent rubric and support. | Tell me about the last course you started and what happened after week two. |

## 05 / Define the curriculum and learning evidence

*Module 3 · Proposed scope, subject to learner and expert review*

The course is for learners with foundations. Eight weeks is a scoped program, not a promise of professional mastery or employment.

| Week | Outcome | Demonstration / assessment |
|---|---|---|
| 0 · prerequisite check | Confirm Python/SQL basics and environment readiness. | Short diagnostic and setup task; route gaps to prework. |
| 1 | Frame a data question and assess data quality. | Document a dataset, risks, missingness and a decision question. |
| 2 | Use SQL/Pandas for reproducible exploration. | Submit a reproducible analysis with units and validation checks. |
| 3 | Apply statistical reasoning and a baseline model. | Explain a baseline and uncertainty; avoid leakage. |
| 4 | Evaluate a predictive model with an appropriate split. | Compare metrics and failure cases on held-out data. |
| 5 | Use GenAI for a bounded analytical workflow. | Compare assisted and unassisted outputs with verification. |
| 6 | Build a small retrieval-grounded workflow. | Document retrieved evidence, limits and unsupported answers. |
| 7 | Evaluate GenAI quality, latency, cost and failure. | Create a small test set and report failure modes. |
| 8 | Communicate and defend the capstone. | Submit reproducible work and respond to rubric-level review. |

**Decision / next validation:** Have a subject-matter reviewer validate scope, correctness and workload. Rubric: problem/data reasoning 25%, method/evaluation 30%, responsible AI/reproducibility 25%, communication 20%; proposed pass ≥70/100.

## 06 / Design the LMS around the learner journey

*Module 3 · Clickable prototype; production integrations not connected*

The academic course is the commercial product; the portal delivers learning and trust. Feature choices should support the learner outcome and viable service costs.

- Platform configuration: learner/instructor/admin roles, course publishing, gradebook and consent-aware event collection.
- MVP trust requirements: access controls, safe payment integration, clear refunds, accessibility and honest outcomes claims.
- Defer mobile apps, enterprise SSO, automatic AI grading and a large course marketplace.

| Journey step | MVP interaction | Success / failure behavior |
|---|---|---|
| Discover | Filter a course path and inspect outcomes, prerequisites and sample lesson. | Clear language, no invented accreditation or placement claims; useful no-results state. |
| Decide | Review syllabus, workload, pilot price and support/refund terms. | Show purchase-relevant information before asking for payment. |
| Enroll | Production: secure checkout and idempotent access grant. | Clear pending/failed states; this site's prototype simulates enrollment only. |
| Learn | Resume lesson, transcript/captions, next task and knowledge check. | Keyboard access, visible focus, saved progress and retryable errors. |
| Demonstrate | View rubric, submit project and see review status. | Clear receipt, permission boundaries and realistic turnaround. |
| Improve | Collect feedback and review learning/acquisition metrics. | Separate quiz attempts, viewed lessons and assessed outcomes. |

**Decision / next validation:** Open the included LMS prototype, then test find-a-course, resume-learning and submit-a-project tasks with real learners.

## 08 / Draft the first ten LMS stories

*Module 4 · Backlog candidates with business value and acceptance criteria*

Read the detailed stories below. They are examples to refine with learners, instructional design and engineering; their technical estimates are not commitments.

### LMS-01 · Understand the course before buying

As a working analyst, I want outcomes, prerequisites, workload, syllabus, sample content and price in one place so I can decide whether this course fits.

**Business value:** Reduce unsuitable purchases and improve qualified conversion.

Acceptance criteria:

1. The page shows eight weeks, 5–7 hours/week, Python/SQL prerequisites and assessment requirements.
2. The pilot price and refund conditions are visible before enrollment.
3. A sample lesson is accessible without a purchase; no placement or accreditation claim is implied.

**Dependencies:** Reviewed proposition, curriculum outline and commercial terms.

### LMS-02 · Enroll with clear payment and access status

As a learner, I want a reliable enrollment confirmation so I know whether I have paid and can access the course.

**Business value:** Support paid conversion without duplicate charges or lost access.

Acceptance criteria:

1. Production uses a vetted payment provider and never stores raw card details in the LMS.
2. A successful provider event grants access once; duplicate events are idempotent.
3. Failed or pending payment shows recovery guidance; confirmation includes the purchase and refund details.

**Dependencies:** Identity/access roles, provider integration, approved terms and security review.

### LMS-03 · Study an accessible lesson

As a time-constrained learner, I want an accessible lesson with a transcript and practice so I can learn in my available study window.

**Business value:** Support learning activation and a credible minimum product.

Acceptance criteria:

1. Lessons include an outcome, readable text, captioned media where used and a worked example.
2. All controls work with a keyboard and have visible focus and labels.
3. A network or content-loading error gives a retry path without losing progress.

**Dependencies:** Approved accessible content and authenticated course access.

### LMS-04 · Resume where I stopped

As a learner, I want the next unfinished lesson and my saved position so I can continue without searching.

**Business value:** Reduce resumption friction and support weekly engagement.

Acceptance criteria:

1. The workspace identifies the last opened and next unfinished lesson.
2. Progress updates persist for the correct learner; learners cannot see another account's records.
3. Completion indicators distinguish viewed content from passed assessments.

**Dependencies:** Course structure, identity and a progress-event definition.

### LMS-05 · Get feedback from a knowledge check

As a learner, I want explanations and retries after a quiz so I can correct misunderstandings.

**Business value:** Improve comprehension rather than rewarding clicks.

Acceptance criteria:

1. Each answer shows why it is correct or incorrect after submission.
2. Retry is allowed and does not overwrite the distinction between an attempt and a final passing result.
3. Question text, options and feedback are keyboard/screen-reader accessible.

**Dependencies:** Assessment blueprint, item review and scoring logic.

### LMS-06 · Submit a project against a visible rubric

As a learner, I want to submit a notebook or repository against clear criteria so I can demonstrate my learning.

**Business value:** Create a credible learning artifact and capstone completion signal.

Acceptance criteria:

1. The rubric is visible before submission and covers analysis, validation, communication and responsible AI use.
2. Allowed link/file types and permissions are explained; malformed submissions show useful errors.
3. Submission receipt and status persist; resubmissions follow a published policy.

**Dependencies:** Project brief, submission storage/access controls and rubric.

### LMS-07 · Receive actionable mentor feedback

As a learner, I want rubric-level feedback so I know what to improve before final review.

**Business value:** Test the feedback differentiator and improve completion quality.

Acceptance criteria:

1. Feedback links to rubric criteria with at least one strength and next action.
2. The promised turnaround is shown and supported by actual mentor capacity.
3. Only the learner and authorized reviewer can access private submissions and feedback.

**Dependencies:** LMS-06, reviewer roles and mentor staffing.

### LMS-08 · Plan study time and office hours

As a working learner, I want a weekly plan and clear office-hour times so I can schedule consistent practice.

**Business value:** Reduce missed support and expectation mismatch.

Acceptance criteria:

1. The plan shows estimated work, due dates and time zone.
2. Office hours show availability and joining instructions.
3. Reminders are opt-in and can be disabled.

**Dependencies:** Curriculum calendar and support process.

### LMS-09 · Find help without losing context

As a blocked learner, I want to ask a question from a lesson so I can recover and keep learning.

**Business value:** Improve recovery and reveal costly recurring content issues.

Acceptance criteria:

1. The help request can include lesson context with learner permission.
2. A help page states response expectations and escalation.
3. Requests are acknowledged and visible only to permitted staff and the learner.

**Dependencies:** Support workflow, staffing and access controls.

### LMS-10 · Understand completion and credential rules

As a learner, I want clear completion criteria and a verifiable record so I understand what I have achieved.

**Business value:** Support truthful outcomes communication and trust.

Acceptance criteria:

1. Completion requires the stated assessments and rubric threshold, not merely video views.
2. Any completion record states issuer and criteria without implying external accreditation.
3. Corrections or revoked records follow an explicit policy.

**Dependencies:** Assessment and completion policy; verified outcome records.

**Decision / next validation:** Keep a definition of ready: evidence or hypothesis, owner, testable criteria, dependencies, assessment/content readiness and estimate confidence.

## 09 / Prioritize with RICE and release gates

*Module 4 · All reach/impact/effort values are illustrative*

Reach is the estimated number of learners, out of one planned 30-learner cohort, that a story would affect during a 90-day pilot window, so some stories reach 18 or 24 rather than all 30. Impact uses one agreed scale; confidence is a fraction in calculation; effort is person-months.

- RICE = reach × impact × confidence ÷ effort. Scores are conversation inputs, not measured business facts.
- Pilot path: course transparency, secure enrollment/access, reviewed lessons, progress, knowledge checks, submission and staffed feedback.
- Mandatory gates override ranking: identity/access isolation, provider-backed payments, refund disclosure, accessibility, content review and sufficient mentor capacity.
- The example scope consumes several person-months in aggregate. Configure existing LMS capabilities to fit the small-team constraint; re-estimate before committing.

| Story | Reach | Impact | Confidence | Effort (person-months) | RICE |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| LMS-01 · Understand the course before buying | 30 | 2 | 80% | 0.75 | 64.0 |
| LMS-05 · Get feedback from a knowledge check | 30 | 2 | 70% | 1 | 42.0 |
| LMS-02 · Enroll with clear payment and access status | 30 | 3 | 70% | 1.5 | 42.0 |
| LMS-04 · Resume where I stopped | 24 | 1 | 80% | 0.5 | 38.4 |
| LMS-03 · Study an accessible lesson | 30 | 3 | 80% | 2 | 36.0 |
| LMS-07 · Receive actionable mentor feedback | 24 | 2 | 60% | 1 | 28.8 |
| LMS-06 · Submit a project against a visible rubric | 24 | 2 | 70% | 1.25 | 26.9 |
| LMS-09 · Find help without losing context | 18 | 1 | 70% | 0.75 | 16.8 |
| LMS-08 · Plan study time and office hours | 18 | 1 | 60% | 0.75 | 14.4 |
| LMS-10 · Understand completion and credential rules | 18 | 1 | 50% | 0.75 | 12.0 |

**Decision / next validation:** Use the live RICE lab to test assumptions. Do not drop a prerequisite because its score is lower than a visible growth feature.

## 10 / Measure the acquisition and learning funnel

*Module 5 · Fictional training dataset, not business performance*

The included Jan–Mar 2026 data is fictional and assumes every eight-week cohort has matured. Units are people/seats and USD, not market-research estimates.

- Acquisition: unique visitors → opted-in qualified leads → paid seats. Keep the attribution window explicit.
- Activation: paid learners attempting the first lesson/check within seven days; track refunded seats consistently.
- Learning success: assessed capstone completion among mature paid cohorts; report non-refunded and all-paid denominators separately.
- Revenue: gross tuition less full refunds. Track payment fees, mentor/platform cost and acquisition spend separately.
- Completion is not a hiring outcome. Do not claim causal feature impact or real demand from this sample.

**Decision / next validation:** Reconcile one funnel aggregate manually. Then choose an experiment: clearer prerequisites, a better first lesson or faster project feedback.

## 11 / Plan revenue and validate willingness to pay

*Module 6 · Illustrative economics — not a forecast*

Pilot assumption: $299 tuition, 30-seat capacity, 8% full refunds, $45 variable delivery cost per seat, 3% processing, $600 acquisition spend and $1,200 fixed campaign/content costs. All require validation.

- Contribution = net tuition − processing on gross tuition − variable delivery − acquisition spend − fixed campaign/content costs.
- Why Accelerated equals Central: demand of 80 seats is capped at the 30-seat delivery capacity, so tuition and contribution stop growing. Extra demand only pays off if capacity, mentors and spend change too.
- The scenario table holds acquisition spend fixed for comparability, which may be unrealistic at higher traffic; change the inputs in the revenue lab.
- This is an incremental planning model, not accounting profit: taxes, founder salary, ongoing overhead, chargebacks and additional content investment may be missing.
- With these assumptions, per-seat contribution before fixed/acquisition costs is $221.11 and illustrative break-even is nine paid seats; ceiling and refund assumptions still matter.

| Campaign scenario | Visitors × lead rate × paid rate | Seats before / after capacity | Net tuition after refunds | Incremental contribution |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Conservative | 2,000 × 5% × 5% | 5 / 5 | $1,375.40 | −$694.45 |
| Central | 5,000 × 8% × 7.5% | 30 / 30 | $8,252.40 | $4,833.30 |
| Accelerated | 8,000 × 10% × 10% | 80 / 30 | $8,252.40 | $4,833.30 |

**Decision / next validation:** Test the proposed price with an honestly described pilot offer. Track actual commitments and refunds; do not take payment before terms, delivery and support are ready.

## 12 / Launch small, learn and revise

*Module 6 · Experiment charter and decision gates*

Recommended next step: a narrow paid-pilot validation, not a large LMS build or broad claim of market leadership.

- Recruit 12 qualified learners plus three buyer interviews; record past purchase behavior and prerequisite readiness.
- Publish a truthful offer and sample lesson. Define channel/measurement before running a small campaign.
- Set the gate in advance: at least 10 qualified paid commitments with acceptable acquisition cost before completing full production; this threshold is a proposed test.
- If interest is high but payment is low, investigate proposition and willingness to pay. If payment is healthy but completion is poor, fix learning/service quality before scaling.
- Review activation, rubric completion, refunds, mentor load and contribution after the mature pilot; log the decision to iterate, stop or expand.

**Decision / next validation:** No live payment or outreach is performed by this learning site. The prototype and calculations let you rehearse the decisions.
